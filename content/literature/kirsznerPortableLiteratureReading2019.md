# Literature notes

Poetry appeals to the senses.

Imagery is language that evokes a physical sensation produced by one or more of the five senses.

Your memory determines how you picture the image triggered by a word.

A word may also have connotations—emotional associations that define your response.

By using images, a poet can accomplish two things:
1. Create pictures in a reader's mind.
2. Create numerous imaginative associations.

Imaginative associations triggered by images establish the atmosphere or mood of a poem.

An image in a poem could trigger different meanings and emotions depending on the reader who reads it.

When a reader reads an image you wrote, they could either:
1. remember it from memory
2. create it in their imagination

Imagery is economic. A few carefully chosen words could evoke a range of emotions and reactions.

Images could assist poets to express abstract ideas (like beauty or love) or make them more understandable to others.
- For example, how can you present beauty through images?

Images can appeal to all senses: sight, hearing, smell, taste, and touch.

A poem may also convey a poet's interpretations of these images.

Imagery could be static (like a painting).

Imagery could be kinetic, conveying a sense of motion or change (like a movie).

Synesthesia occurs when one sense is described in terms of another sense (e.g., describe sound using color).
- I am feeling blue.
- You look hot!
- This song is smooth.

Questions to analyze a poem's images.
