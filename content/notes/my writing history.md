# Poetry

As a child, my only exposure to poems growing up was through the Old Testament's poetic books like Psalms, Proverbs, and the Songs of Solomon. At school, I also encountered *bugtong* (riddles), *salawikain* (proverbs), and *sawikain* (sayings).

In high school, I wrote lyrics for a classmate's original guitar tunes. Those lyrics were very symbolic, almost undecipherable. But I wrote them anyway and sang them. I remember a classmate who listened to me sing one of my songs said she didn't understood it but, somehow, the musicality of it all—sound and lyrics—made her cry. Other than this, I never really wrote poems as a young person.

The earliest poem I may have written was [[tribute to the clouds of south cotabato|Tribute to the Clouds of South Cotabato]], which I wrote while riding a plane to Manila after attending a national writing contest held in Mindanao in 2008. I can't remember writing other poems after this. Poems never really interest me enough to get me deep into them.

However, I always had a mood in me. I always associate this mood with deep thinking and feeling. Somehow, it may have led me to depression. But it is always here and when I'm in that mood I'm able to write and usually whatever comes out, whether poetry or prose, is musical, poetic, or lyrical.

My next exposure to poetry I could remember is through [[dave ursillo|Dave Ursillo]], whom I discovered around 2015. I read his poetry books and read the [[tzuTaoTeChing2006|Tao te Ching]] and the [[easwaranBhagavadGita2009|Bhagavad Gita]] through his recommendation. These ancient spiritual texts were written in verse. I read them about three years after I left my religion when I discovered that I was still spiritual and wanted to explore a form of spirituality that wasn't dependent on the existence of a God. I never wrote poems here, though. I just read them.

My more proper introduction to poetry was through [[rem tanauan|Rem Tanauan]]. Shortly after I met him in circa 2017, I attended a night of reading Rumi's poems where he was one of the readers. After that event, he invited me to join FLOW, a writing group exploring writing alongside spirituality. This was when, I think I may have started dipping my toes more into writing poetry. But at this time, I still considered myself an essayist and I felt more at home there.

Rem and I will continue to nurture this friendship and I would have several long conversations with him about writing and poetry. Around this time, I would discover Thoreau, who wasn't a great poet but whose poetry emanates from his prose. I became more in love with walking because of Thoreau and in many of those walks, I wrote poems.

A few months before the pandemic, Rem and I had conversations more targeted on the kind of poetry he resonates with and a poetics that could articulate it. We both called it Poetikang Ginhawa (Ginhawa Poetics).

When the pandemic began in 2020, Rem, without telling me beforehand, started his Tungko ng Tula, where he became my teacher and no longer a conversation partner. Knowing I have almost zero knowledge of poetry, I joined and my friend became my teacher. This was when I gained a deeper appreciation of poetry and a bit of technical knowledge of the craft. At the same time, I discovered how poetry could be fun. I wrote a lot of humorous poems and read them in class. I also discovered that I could play with forms. I did blackout poems, after Austin Kleon and shared those, too.

After Tungko ended, I continued to write my poems. It wasn't still a practice unlike my journaling. But I wrote poems more frequently because of the class. When I transferred to Los Baños, I began writing poems about the place—poems that always came during walks. Rem and I continued to converse about poetry even if I chose to distance myself from his Tungko project, which was growing fast. He started talking about it as a movement rather than just a group—a movement of democratized poetry, made up of enthusiasts and amateurs. Conversations there had clear anti-institution overtones.

A week before Rem died, I met Rofel Brion, whose poetry represented a more formal model of what Rem was trying to do: spiritual poems easy to understand and communicated in an accessible form. I joined Rofel's workshop in San Pablo City. That encounter was my first poetry instruction from a distinguished and credentialed poet from the academe.

Since these encounters, I started calling myself a writer who writes essays and poems. Even before Rem died, I already imbibed this identity.

My poetry has received institutional validation only in 2024 after a few poems landed me a spot at the Palihang Rogelio Sicat and my collection of Pangasinan poems won third place in the Gawad F Sionil Jose.

I still consider myself a newbie, though. Without much institutional training and comprehensive instruction, my poetry practice lacks a strong foundation to follow a traditional path. At the same time, however, I'm unable to make it flourish by myself since I don't have a strong poetics that will guide me in my practice. Thus this attempt in articulation.
