A system to design a life dynamically, changing whenever necessary and ever-evolving, has the following:

1. A [[life design assessment]] and a list of life design problems based on the life design assessment.
2. A [[my compass|compass]], which is a congruent [[workview]] (see [[my workview]]) and [[lifeview]] (see [[my lifeview]]).
3. A [[good time journal]] that clearly identifies the activities that lead you to joy (see [[my good time journal]]). When doing this for the first time, it might also be helpful to do the [[good time journal mind map exercise]].
4. A set of [[odyssey plans]].
5. A set of [[prototype questions]] based on the odyssey plans.
6. A set of [[prototype experiences]] based on the odyssey plans.

When going through this process for the first time, all of these elements must be in place. However, if one has already "committed" to a certain shape of life that one only has to build upon through relatively smaller changes or acts of risk-taking, one doesn't have to do a new good time journal or create new odyssey plans. I found that these two elements are more applicable when one is going through a big, life-changing process. If one isn't in that place, introducing prototypes to improve one's life or make smaller pivots and making sure these prototypes are aligned with one's compass is the way to go.

# How to make life design choices

Throughout the life design process, you will make several choices. A choice means crossing out an option and committing to one. The process of making life design choices involve the following steps:

1. **Gather and create some options.** All the elements of a life design are meant to gather choices. One's workview, lifeview, good-time journals, mind maps, odyssey plans, and prototype conversations and experiences are all meant to generate excellent options.
2. **Narrow down your list to your top alternatives (3-5 options).** You can narrow down by grouping your options into larger categories and then choosing your top contender for each category. From here, choose 3–5 options.
3. **Choose discerningly.** After doing the logical work, access other forms of knowing, including your intuition, emotion, gut feel, social knowing, kinesthetic knowing, spiritual knowing, etc. to make a choice. You can also use grokking, which means living out a choice for about 1–3 days.
4. **Let go and move on.**

The key to making life design choices or commitments is to test the waters first through prototypes before making significant investments in time, money, and other resources.

If you've already committed to a particular shape of life (a large commitment), you can simply see your next choices as smaller enhancements to that larger life. You will only have to go through a more complex process when faced with life-changing events or choices.
