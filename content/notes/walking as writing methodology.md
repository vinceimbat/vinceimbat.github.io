There are different ways to walk with the goal of writing
- pure photography
- pure description of what one sees (using mindfulness) — see [[having listened whited|Having Listened by Gary Whited]]
- free writing (what comes: thoughts, observations)
- pure memory
- interviews or ethographic
- historical (usually involves reading about the place first before walking)
- day-by-day returning

What is it with walking that makes it special?

- directness, being there vs. mediated experience of place
- experiencing the body as one experiences place (its limits but also how place and other people there respond to one's body when it moves through place)
- POV of the pedestrian: is this place walkable, livable (important because more in more places are designed for cars than the pedestrian)
- walking is "current" (now-ness)—a snapshot—which responds to the dynamic nature of places. You can describe a place now but it could be different later.
