My father said it is impossible to  
forget my birthday because  
it was the same day a favorite  
uncle died. I was born in  
the morning, he died in the evening,  
my birth, his clarion call  
to heaven.

But on the next dinner, my aunt  
tells it different. Said the uncle  
died first in the morning and I  
was born in the evening. There are  
inevitable lies in this poem  
but I have no use of these stories  
anyway. I haven't celebrated  
birthdays half my life.

Somewhere, right now, a child  
is born. At the same time,  
a child dies. From an unavoidable  
disease, from a shrapnel, from  
a rogue bullet. We do not  
ask when one life ends and  
when one begins. We do not think  
about these things. Over dinner.

We prefer to talk about the weather,  
about work, about an increasing  
salary—the in-between events  
of two breaths.
