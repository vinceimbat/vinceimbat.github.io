_after Esteban Pichay Villanueva's The Basi Revolt (1821)_

The sound of horseshoes  
on stone lures me to slumber  
as I stare at this lampshade,  
a volcano of amber lava upwelling  
on its crater, the debris  
of a promise I once made  
to a girl who lives in this city.

We finish high school now, but  
in three years we shall meet again,  
I told her, a promise I was eager to fulfill  
the first time I returned to this city.

In between visits to a nearby town  
where a river once turned red  
like *basi*, I waited for her response.  
But a fern is not a comet.  
And on that afternoon,  
the sky like a phone screen  
was an omen of blue paper  
soaked in vinegar.

My revolt against a prohibition  
to fall in love with an unbeliever  
ended on that long bridge,  
the city leaving nothing  
but arrows buried in chests,  
severed heads inside bird cages,  
bodies hanging from the gallows.
