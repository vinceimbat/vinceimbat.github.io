## Take the train

Suvarnabhumi airport has a train line that connects it to the city center. It's very efficient kahit malaki luggage mo, and you'll save a minimum of 300 baht. The airport train line connects to the BTS train that passes by different parts of Bangkok. 

## Park

I lived near Kasetsart University north of Bangkok. Malayo na siya sa town, pero 4 train stops away from where I lived is the Wachirabenchathat Park or Rot Fai Park. Lagi ako sa park na ito. It's a wide green area composed of three adjacent parks -- Chatuchak Park, Queen Sirikit Park, and Wachirabenchathat. There are walking places and sitting places all over. Go there around 4pm and the park is filled with joggers/ walkers/ bikers/ families with children. Sana maabutan mo yung mga pink flowering trees. 

To get there, take the BTS train and get down at Chatuchak station or MRT train and get down at Mo Chit station. Enter the area via Chatuchak Park and the walking trails will lead you to the Rot Fai Park. 

## Budhadhasa Indapano Archive

https://en.bia.or.th/bia

At the other end of the Rot Fai Park is this Buddhist center and library. You can enter the place free. If you're in Bangkok at the first Sunday of March, you can join the Taste of Liberation free event (<https://en.bia.or.th/taste-of-liberation>](https://en.bia.or.th/taste-of-liberation)). It's a half day introduction on the monk behind the center, meditation and ends with joint community lunch with monks. The introduction is in English and done by volunteers.

## Loy La Long

https://loylalong.com/

I once staycationed at an AirBnB house called Loy La Long overlooking the Chao Praya River. There's a narrow corridor that leads to it and dilapidated wood planks separate it from the next house that looks equally old (I could hear the neighbor frying their meal at dinner time). Some people wouldn't like this, pero charming yung mga ganitong old houses that are maintained and have history to tell. Not the mention the view. 

## Old part of Bangkok

The area where Loy La Long is situated is the old part of Bangkok. It's nice to just walk around the historical neighborhood of mixed Thai, Chinese and Portuguese influences. Buddhist temples, Chinese pagodas and a Catholic Church co-exist. One interesting part of the neighborhood is Talad Noi. Not sure again whether this is your kind of thing. It's not a 'beautified' neighborhood -- houses are old and it's known for shops selling junks (second hand auto parts). But it's also littered with heritage houses and nice cafés. It's a community-driven tourism effort, and feeling ko ang concept nila is that they present it as it is rather than go the usual tourism route of painting unsightly walls with visually appealing colors. I thought it's quite interesting. 

The MRT station of Hua Lamphong is your gateway to the old part of Bangkok.

## Chao Phraya

If you want to experience riverine Bangkok, take the Chao Phraya Ferry at Sathorn Pier. The pier is just outside of Saphan Taksin BTS train station. You can get down at the Wat Arun stop which is in front of the beautiful Wat Arun temple. The temple is illuminated in gold by the evening and maganda siyang tingnan from the ferry. May ferry din sa Wat Arun the goes to the other side of the river, and you can walk from there to Wat Pho temple. The Wat Pho temple is situated still in the old part of Bangkok. 

## Ayutthaya

Take the train from Bangkok to Ayuthaya. There are different kinds of train; the ordinary one is super cheap but fine and quite an experience. 

I stayed at a bed and breakfast ([Athithara Homestay](https://www.athitharahomestay.com/) -- 29 Soi U-thong 21 T Pratuchai) a bit far from the center kaya kailangan mag-Grab or tuktuk. I was attracted to the traditional wooden Thai houses and has a view of Chao Phraya. 

If you're going to look at this place in the map, you will notice there are temple ruins at the other side of the main road. There is a walking trail there (see photo below) and it will lead you from one ruin to another. Well-developed but silent and empty ang trail na ito kasi nasa center karamihan ng tao. This is the best find I had in Ayutthaya. Just following this trail made me understand yung description ng Ayutthaya as advanced in using water and ditches in its landscape architecture.
