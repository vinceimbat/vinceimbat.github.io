# Proofreading checklist

- Review front matter. Ensure that the front matter matches the design sample and the latest copyedited manuscript version.
- Review TOC. Ensure that TOC elements match what is inside the book.
	- Check chapter numberings.
	- Check subsection numberings.
	- Ensure consistent heading capitalization.
		- Go through headings in TOC.
		- Go through the headings in the book.
- Review figures and tables.
	- Go through figure and table numbers
	- Go through figure and table names.
	- Go through figure and table captions.
- Go through notes and references.
- Go through the word list.
- Read the book.
	- Review the style sheet before reading
	- Then read slowly.

## Advice from Cinque

the purpose of the proofread is to be the final reader of the pages to make sure that everything is free of mistakes and has been set correctly in the book.

you will be concerned with making sure that the pages have no remaining errors and that everything has been set properly.

You are the final reader; there is no reader after you. So if anything is incorrect or incomplete, you are the person to correct it (or at least draw someone's attention to it.)

you should not second-guess style or writing decisions.

The goal is not to improve the book as an editor might do; the goal is to make sure it is clean and without errors.

## Checklist

if you see a placeholder in the text such as "See p. 000" you will need to find and indicate which page is being referred to.

**Spelling and grammar must be made consistent and correct.**

1. "tight rope" vs. "tight-rope" vs. "tightrope"
2. "Civil Rights Movement" vs. "civil rights movement"
3. 1970s vs. 1970's vs. '70s vs. '70s
4. "that" vs "which" according to American usage
5. "blonde" vs. "blond"
6. "Resume" is incorrect if the correct spelling is "résumé"

**References**

1. Confirm all references and citations are correctly formatted and punctuated, per examples on the style sheet and per TNP house style.

**Typesetting and layout**

1. All design elements consistent
2. Check for bad/awkward line breaks and page breaks such as splitting the name "Facebook" across lines as "Faceb-ook" or splitting the title of a poem across the page from the body of the poem.
3. All art placed correctly and appropriately based on where the references to the art are located in the text.
4. Mark lines that are set too tight or too loose.
5. Mark unexplained spaces, things that look like they are set in the wrong place, etc.

**Internal nomenclature consistency**

All elements such as chapter titles, section titles, book parts, etc. should be internally consistent, i.e., a chapter should not be called "Trees: A Meditation" in the table of contents and "Trees--a Meditation" in the chapter itself. The text should not refer to "Image 9" when the image itself is labeled "Figure 9". These should be consistent. In that regard, note that all of the following would be considered different and would need to be made consistent:

    - Figure 9.
    - Figure 9
    - Fig. 9
    - Fig. 9:
    - _Figure_ 9
    - figure 9
    - and so on

**Queries**

perhaps the same paragraph is repeated verbatim in two different chapters. Please certainly do query those kinds of problems.

**Others**

Subheads misaligned?
Pages run short?
